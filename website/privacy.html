Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-08-03

Short version

RNP collects no data about you. We have no servers storing your data. The app works entirely on-device, with network calls only when you explicitly fetch a key from a keyserver or WKD.

Long version

Data stored on your Mac

  • Your OpenPGP keyring (public and secret keys) in the App Group container
  • Your keyring passphrase in the macOS Keychain (Touch ID-protected if enabled)
  • App preferences (selected tab, signing key, etc.) in ~/Library/Preferences/com.rnpgp.RNPForMail.plist

None of this is ever sent anywhere.

Network calls RNP makes

  • WKD lookup — when you search for a recipient key by email, RNP queries https://openpgpkey.<domain>/.well-known/openpgpkey/.... This goes to the recipient's own domain.
  • keys.openpgp.org — only when you explicitly search the keyserver or upload your own key. Never automatic.
  • Sparkle update check — weekly check against https://rnpgp.org/updates/appcast.xml. Disabled by toggling the auto-update setting in Tools.

What we don't do

  • No analytics. No usage tracking. No event collection.
  • No crash reporting. (MacOS's own crash reporter may ask you; that's separate from RNP.)
  • No advertising SDKs.
  • No third-party frameworks that phone home.

Mail extension

The Mail extension processes message bodies locally to detect PGP/MIME structure and decrypt them. Decrypted content stays in your Mac's memory; nothing is sent over the network from the Mail extension except Autocrypt header gossip (part of the protocol) and keyserver lookups you initiate.

Source code

You don't have to take our word for it. RNP is open source: github.com/rnpgp/rnp-mailapp-extension. Audit it yourself.
